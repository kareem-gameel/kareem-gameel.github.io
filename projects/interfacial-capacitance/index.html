01 · First-principles interfaces

Interfacial Capacitance at the Nanoscale

Connecting atomistic electronic structure, electrostatic screening, and ion confinement to the capacitance measured in low-dimensional energy-storage interfaces.

  • Density Functional Theory
  • Quantum Capacitance
  • Graphene Interfaces
  • Nanopore Confinement

Research question

How can first-principles electronic structure and classical electrostatics be combined to predict the measurable capacitance of interfaces whose dimensions approach the atomic scale? In graphene and other low-dimensional electrodes, charge storage depends not only on geometry and dielectric screening, but also on the finite electronic density of states and the energetic cost of accumulating charge.

Physical framework

I developed the Quantum Interfacial Capacitance framework to describe electrostatic and quantum contributions within one first-principles model. The approach resolves how field-induced charge redistribution, interfacial screening, and electronic structure jointly determine the capacitance-voltage response. Applied to graphene/silica interfaces, it explains nonlinear trends that conventional electrostatic or isolated quantum-capacitance descriptions do not capture.

Extreme confinement

A complementary study examines ions inside graphene slit pores, where atomistic structure and ion-electrode interactions depart strongly from bulk behavior. Modeling this regime identifies how pore width, electronic response, and confined ion configurations alter charge storage and helps connect microscopic descriptors to experimentally relevant electrode performance.

Why it matters

Predictive interface models can guide the design of batteries, supercapacitors, sensors, catalysts, and semiconductor devices. The broader objective is to determine which physical ingredients are required for accuracy, then retain those ingredients in computational models that can scale across materials and realistic interface structures.
