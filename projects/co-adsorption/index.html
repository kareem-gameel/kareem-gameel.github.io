02 · Surface electronic structure

Electronic Origins of CO Adsorption on Metal Surfaces

Using first-principles descriptors and molecular-orbital analysis to explain CO chemisorption trends on nickel and copper surfaces.

  • Surface Chemistry
  • CO Chemisorption
  • Density Functional Theory
  • Molecular Orbitals

Research question

What electronic interactions govern the strength and preferred configuration of CO adsorption on transition-metal surfaces? The long-standing CO adsorption puzzle shows that numerical agreement alone is not enough: a predictive description requires identifying how the electronic structure of the surface controls chemisorption.

Molecular-orbital perspective

I used first-principles calculations together with molecular-orbital analysis to resolve the donation and back-donation interactions that shape CO bonding on copper surfaces. This interpretation connects changes in adsorption behavior to physically meaningful orbital interactions instead of treating computed energies as isolated outputs.

Electronic descriptors

The work was extended across nickel and copper surfaces to identify descriptors that connect substrate electronic structure with CO chemisorption. Comparing metals and surface environments reveals which electronic quantities organize adsorption trends and where simplified models of surface reactivity succeed or fail.

Why it matters

Adsorption is central to heterogeneous catalysis, sensing, and surface processing. Physically interpretable descriptors help translate first-principles calculations into design rules, making electronic-structure theory more useful for comparing catalytic materials and understanding reaction interfaces.
