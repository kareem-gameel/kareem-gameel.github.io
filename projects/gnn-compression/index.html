04 · Efficient graph learning

Neuron-Level Sparsity and Efficient GNN Compression

Transforming overparameterized molecular graph neural networks into smaller, faster models while preserving predictive accuracy and generalization.

  • Structured Pruning
  • Neuron-Level Sparsity
  • Molecular GNNs
  • Efficient ML

Research question

How much of a trained molecular graph neural network is actually required for accurate prediction? Modern atomistic models are often overparameterized, increasing memory use, training cost, and inference time even when many hidden units contribute little to the final prediction.

Neuron-level compression

This project develops structured pruning methods that identify and remove redundant neurons rather than isolated scalar weights. Neuron-level sparsity changes the physical dimensions of network layers, so successful pruning can produce compact models that execute efficiently on standard hardware without requiring specialized sparse-matrix kernels.

Performance criteria

Compression is evaluated as a multi-objective problem. The relevant measures include parameter count, model size, peak memory, computational operations, and inference time alongside predictive error. Transfer tests determine whether a compressed model retains the representations needed to generalize beyond its training distribution rather than merely preserving in-distribution accuracy.

Research objective

The goal is to understand where redundancy appears in molecular GNNs and convert that understanding into reliable compression strategies. Smaller models can lower the cost of large-scale screening, accelerate simulation workflows, and make advanced scientific ML practical in resource-constrained research and industrial settings.
