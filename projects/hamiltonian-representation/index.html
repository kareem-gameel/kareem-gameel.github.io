03 · Electronic representation

Hamiltonian-Informed Electronic Structure Learning

Combining electronic-structure-derived descriptors with atomistic geometry to learn bandgaps and spectral quantities governed by nonlocal electronic coupling.

  • Hamiltonian Descriptors
  • Bandgap Prediction
  • Graph Neural Networks
  • Multimodal Learning

Research question

When is molecular or crystal geometry alone insufficient for learning an electronic property? Local atomistic graph representations are powerful, but global observables such as bandgaps and densities of states can depend on orbital interactions and long-range electronic couplings that are not exposed directly by coordinates and species.

Representation design

The electronic Hamiltonian provides a physically structured description of orbital energies and couplings. This project investigates descriptors derived from Hamiltonian information and combines them with conventional atomistic graph representations. The objective is not to replace geometry, but to determine which electronic features complement it for targets controlled by nonlocal electronic structure.

Multimodal learning

Geometry and Hamiltonian information are encoded as distinct, physically meaningful modalities and fused within a graph-based prediction model. Comparisons against geometry-only and electronic-only baselines reveal when each representation contributes, whether the combined model improves sample efficiency, and how well the learned mapping transfers to unfamiliar chemical structures.

Research objective

The immediate target is accurate bandgap prediction. The broader direction extends to global spectral quantities such as the density of states and ultimately to transferable models of interfacial electronic response. The work aims to establish practical criteria for matching a physical representation to the electronic observable being learned.
